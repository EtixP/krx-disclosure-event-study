# M2.3 — Prospective outcome evaluator

## Objective

Attach realized future observations to an immutable M2.2 decision without
putting any future value into the decision or blending prospective results with
historical research.

## Evaluation boundary

M2.3 evaluates individual eligible decisions only. It does not aggregate an
experiment, apply minimum event/issuer counts, classify frozen success/failure
criteria, or produce a report; those are M2.4 responsibilities. Rejected
decisions remain visible in M2.2 but have no simulated trade outcome.

The evaluator accepts source-bound `m2.3-daily-close-v1` observations. Each
record contains instrument identity, market, trading date, finite positive
close, source/version, UTC availability and capture timestamps, and a SHA-256
recomputed from that complete normalized record. This is deliberately not
described as a hash of unavailable provider raw bytes. Raw prospective market
capture is a later roadmap responsibility.

## Frozen rule application

The first supported rule family is deliberately narrow:

- entry: `next_trading_day_close`, version `m2.1-v1`, with integer
  `session_offset >= 1`;
- exit: `trading_session_close`, version `m2.1-v1`, with integer
  `holding_sessions >= 1`.

Unknown rule IDs, versions, parameters, benchmark semantics, cost models, or
tax-policy versions fail explicitly. The frozen benchmark's dated observations
define market sessions from the trigger's Korean filing date. Entry and exit
are selected by the frozen offsets, then stock closes are required on those
exact dates. This avoids shifting a horizon merely because a stock row is
missing or the issuer was suspended.

Benchmark observations must match the decision's exact market mapping, source,
version, price-return type, exact-date alignment, and fail-on-missing policy.
Stock entry and exit must use one source/version. All selected observations must
become available after the decision and be captured by the evaluation cutoff.
The cutoff is the latest selected capture timestamp, making retries from the
same evidence byte-identical even when invoked later.

## Return and cost derivation

The outcome stores and revalidates:

- stock gross return: `stock_exit / stock_entry - 1`;
- benchmark gross return: `benchmark_exit / benchmark_entry - 1`;
- dated roundtrip cost fraction from the exact frozen M0.2 commission, VAT,
  per-side slippage, and verified Korean transaction-tax policy;
- raw net return: `stock gross - cost`;
- abnormal net return: `stock gross - benchmark gross - cost`.

The complete entry/exit rules, benchmark definition, and cost assumptions are
copied into the outcome and reconciled against the stored decision. A
self-consistent outcome using altered assumptions is rejected by the ledger.

## Separate append-only storage

`forward_event_outcomes` is distinct from `forward_event_decisions`. A composite
foreign key binds each outcome to the exact decision ID/hash, and there is at
most one outcome per decision and experiment/version/trigger identity. Canonical
JSON, outcome hashes, redundant columns, and the decision reference are checked
on every read.

Update and delete triggers make outcomes append-only; recursive triggers also
block `INSERT OR REPLACE` conflict deletion. Exact retries return the existing
record, while a different observation vintage conflicts rather than rewriting
the first prospective result. The decision row is never updated and contains no
price, return, or outcome columns. Every outcome carries
`sample_kind="prospective_forward"`, `is_outcome=true`, `is_decision=false`, and
`authorizes_execution=false`.

## Independent verification failure

The separation, cost arithmetic, decision foreign key, and append-only storage
worked as documented, but the frozen market-time boundary did not.

First, `DailyCloseObservation` only requires `captured_at >= available_at`; it
does not relate either timestamp to `trading_date`. Rehashed observations for
all future sessions, including the September 23 exit, were accepted with
availability and capture on September 16 immediately after the decision. With
the evaluator clock still on September 16, M2.3 produced an outcome whose exit
date was seven days in the future.

Second, the input benchmark rows are treated as a complete trading calendar
without a dataset identity or completeness proof. Removing the real September
18 session and appending September 24 kept the required row count and silently
shifted the frozen exit from September 23 to September 24, contradicting the
frozen horizon and `missing_data_policy="fail"`.

Third, session indexing is wrong when the disclosure date is not itself a
trading session. For a Saturday, September 19 disclosure and the following
Monday-through-Monday calendar, `next_trading_day_close` with
`session_offset=1` selected Tuesday, September 22 rather than Monday,
September 21. The current implementation filters sessions on or after the event
date and indexes at one, rather than counting one session strictly after it.

Finally, the persistence boundary does not prove rule application. A forged
outcome substituted September 18 entry observations for the correct September
17 observations, recomputed every observation hash, return, cost, and outcome
hash, and retained the exact frozen entry/exit rule objects. `ForwardOutcome`
validated it and `ForwardOutcomeLedger.record()` durably stored it. The ledger
checks that assumptions equal the decision, but the stored outcome contains no
complete canonical session evidence from which those dates can be re-derived.

Focused M2.3, combined M2.1–M2.3/M1.2, and full suites still passed `6/6`,
`78/78`, and `383/383`. Scoped Black, compilation, CLI/import smoke,
`git diff --check`, SQLite integrity, and foreign-key checks passed. M0.4,
M0.5, and M1.1 regenerated byte-for-byte at `1acf9366…`, `3677a4c2…`, and
`33ff2517…`; the pinned database remained `265d888a…`.

M2.3 returns to `IN PROGRESS`. It needs trading-date-aware availability,
authoritative complete session evidence, correct non-trading-day offset
semantics, and a persistence invariant that re-derives selected dates from that
evidence. M2.4 was not started.

## Builder correction after verification failure

Daily-close validation now rejects availability before the observation's Korean
trading date. At outcome validation, each required close must additionally be
available no earlier than the exact session close recorded by the calendar.
This prevents a rehashed future close from maturing an outcome early.

The benchmark rows no longer define the calendar. M2.3 now accepts a distinct
`m2.3-session-calendar-v1` record containing market and symbol identity,
source/version, a raw source-payload digest, exact session close timestamps, a
complete window, and capture time. Every weekday in the window must be
classified exactly once as an open session or explicit closure, and the record
cannot be captured before its final represented session closes. Its normalized
contents have a recomputable SHA-256. This provides the complete source-bound
session evidence needed to distinguish a missing price from a closed market.

Offsets are applied to sessions strictly after the trigger's Korean filing
date. Thus `session_offset=1` means Monday for a Saturday disclosure. The
outcome embeds every benchmark observation from the first post-event session
through the frozen exit, not merely the selected endpoints. Missing September
18 benchmark evidence now fails without moving the September 23 exit.

`ForwardOutcome` re-applies the frozen rule parser to its embedded calendar,
requires the complete benchmark prefix to match that calendar, and checks the
selected stock/benchmark entry and exit against the derived indices. The ledger
canonicalizes through this schema before writing, so a fully rehashed and
arithmetically self-consistent September 18→23 substitute is rejected and no
row is committed.

## Independent re-verification failure after the correction

The four original attacks now fail as intended, but the replacement calendar
boundary is not actually bound to its claimed raw source. The schema accepts a
caller-provided `source_payload_sha256`, normalized sessions and closures, and a
recomputed normalized `calendar_sha256`; it neither stores the source payload
nor invokes a source-specific parser that can prove the normalized dates came
from those bytes.

An independent attack classified the real Friday, September 18 session as an
explicit closure, appended Thursday, September 24 as an open session, and
recomputed the calendar hash. The object still classified every weekday and
passed all calendar validation. The evaluator selected September 17→24 instead
of the real September 17→23 frozen horizon, and the outcome ledger durably
stored the result. Thus `complete=true` and an unchecked digest only attest to a
self-consistent caller assertion; they do not distinguish missing benchmark
data from an actual exchange closure.

Focused M2.3, combined M1.2/M2.1–M2.3, and full suites passed `10/10`, `82/82`,
and `387/387`; the stronger closure-substitution path is uncovered. The three

diff, SQLite integrity, and foreign-key checks also passed.

## Second builder correction — retained official KRX calendar

The calendar trust root is now independent of the Naver benchmark-price rows.
On 2026-09-15, the Builder retrieved KRX's official annual holiday grid with
`search_bas_yy=2026` and `gridTp=KRX`. The exact 2,049-byte UTF-8 response is
retained in `kdtb.data.krx_calendar` at SHA-256
`89ccce131de8d0c4baa6a30d62b7d2e8e3bdc872c71a21d7d81d4b667330d384`.
The source page is KRX's `MKD01100305` holiday calendar. A final fresh retrieval
matched all 2,049 bytes and the full digest.

The versioned parser requires that exact pinned body for the 2026 evidence
version, decodes strict JSON, and validates the `block1` envelope plus every
`calnd_dd`, `calnd_dd_dy`, `dy_tp_cd`, `kr_dy_tp`, and `holdy_nm` field. Dates
must be unique, ascending, internally consistent, and in the requested year.
An updated provider body or later year must receive a new retained source and
parser version; it cannot silently change an existing outcome's semantics.

`TradingSessionCalendar` now embeds the retained UTF-8 body and verifies its
digest before calling that parser. It derives the complete weekday partition
from the parsed official holidays and derives each regular close at 15:30 KST.
The normalized `sessions` and `closed_weekdays` fields must exactly equal this
derivation. `ForwardOutcome` embeds the complete calendar, and both evaluator
input canonicalization and ledger canonicalization re-run the same validation.

The verifier's substitution is now contradicted by the source itself: the KRX
payload identifies September 24 and 25 as Chuseok closures, while September 18
is an ordinary session. Reclassifying September 18 as closed and adding
September 24 fails even after recomputing the normalized calendar hash. Removing
September 24 from the retained JSON and recomputing both the source and calendar
hashes fails the pinned-byte check. Neither path writes an outcome row.

The corrected official calendar preserves the September 17→23 weekday example.
For the Saturday, September 19 disclosure, entry remains Monday, September 21;
the two real Chuseok closures mean that four holding sessions mature on
September 29 rather than September 25. Only the pinned 2026 source version is
currently supported, matching the active experiment's 2026 evaluation window.

## Independent verification failure — exceptional session hours

`89ccce131de8d0c4baa6a30d62b7d2e8e3bdc872c71a21d7d81d4b667330d384`.
Reclassification of September 18, insertion of September 24, mutation of the
retained body, and recomputation of the relevant hashes all fail. The prior
calendar-completeness blocker is corrected.

The response itself is only a holiday list: its fields contain dates, weekday
