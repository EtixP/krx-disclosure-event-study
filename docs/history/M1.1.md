# M1.1 — Canonical economic-event model and normalization

## Objective

Create one inspectable representation for an underlying economic event without
silently changing the verified filing-level research or prematurely building a
live watcher/storage system.

## Evidence before design

The pinned local database has 1,209,249 disclosures from 2021-06-28 through
2026-08-26 (SHA-256
`265d888ac2ba4e205c95a1611e0ba260dbea5e5a5237761995bbd9444fef5137`).
The deterministic prevalence audit found:

- 203,626 OPEN DART provider-prefixed updates (16.84% of the corpus);
- 196,363 corrections, 7,191 other amendments, and 72 correction requests;
- 5,281 titles containing an explicit withdrawal/cancellation/termination
  marker;
- 54,526 same-issuer/title/date collision groups containing 196,387 rows.

Those collision counts measure normalization pressure; they do not prove that
any two receipts are duplicates. The report is
`artifacts/m1_1/normalization_prevalence.json` (SHA-256 `33ff2517…`).

The official
[OPEN DART disclosure-list guide](https://opendart.fss.or.kr/guide/detail.do?apiGrpCd=DS001&apiId=2019001)
documents update prefixes and `rm` flags, but its list response does not return
the original receipt number. The public DART report viewer exposes stronger
relations: the exact revision `family`, attachment history under `att`, and
broader related documents under `ref`. An attachment page can expose the
requested receipt under `att` while `family` contains its parent report chain.

A live falsification demonstrated why title heuristics are unacceptable.
Correction `20260826900745` has a nearer prior same-issuer/same-title filing
`20251208900227`, but DART's family identifies the chain
`20260826900745 → 20260109900166 → 20230328901358 → 20220110900219`.
The 2025 receipt is a different economic event. Separately, withdrawal
`20260326000528` links through DART's related-document list to correction
`20260224003990`, whose family traces to original `20260209000679`.

## Design chosen

`EconomicEvent` contains the roadmap target fields plus `lineage_status` and a
normalization version. Every observed filing remains in `source_provenance`
with its receipt, source timestamp, action, relationship type, raw URL, DART
remarks, and deterministic raw-payload hash. Normalized fields are applied in
source chronology, so an amendment can supersede a field without overwriting
the original record.

`DartClient.fetch_report_relations` retrieves the viewer page and parses exact
receipt IDs from `family`, `att`, and `ref`. The requested attachment and parent
family returned on one page form an authoritative bridge. The relation schema
requires a matching DART viewer URL and raw-response SHA-256, which are carried
into event provenance for each linked filing. A malformed/provider-error or
unevidenced response fails instead of returning an empty relationship. Revision
families and attachments are always eligible. Broader related documents are
used only for explicit cancellation or completion chains to reduce accidental
over-grouping.

`normalize_disclosures` is the only normalization boundary. Historical callers
pass a batch; a future live caller passes the currently observed affected
component after persisting raw inputs. Both paths therefore execute identical
classification, graph construction, chronology, merging, and status logic.

Relationship receipt IDs later than the source receipt are ignored. This stops
a DART page fetched today from importing a future family member into an earlier
replay snapshot. Missing relationship data is represented as `unresolved`; it
never falls back to nearest-date or same-title assignment. Known relationships
that omit source rows remain `partial`. Cross-issuer connections fail.

The canonical ID is `dart:<primary_receipt_no>`, where the primary is the
earliest authoritative receipt in the connected relationship component.

## Historical compatibility

Verified event-study CSVs remain one row per disclosure receipt and all prior
research artifacts remain immutable. `project_legacy_event_study_rows` keeps
the existing query shape, row cardinality, and fixed-width identifiers; callers
may opt in to an additional canonical ID explicitly. M1.1 does not retroactively
deduplicate or reinterpret any headline sample.

## Remaining limitations and handoff invariants

- Historical DART relationship pages have not been bulk-fetched or persisted.
- A self-keyed `unresolved` update has a provisional event ID. Future
  persistence must merge/rekey it (and retain an alias) when authoritative
  lineage later reveals an earlier primary receipt.
- The viewer is a public HTML surface, not an OPEN DART JSON field. Future raw
  ingestion should retain the fetched relationship evidence and expose parser
  failures operationally.
- DART list timestamps are date-only unless separately enriched; normalization
  does not fabricate intraday precision.
- `rm` flags can describe later provider state, so they are retained as
  provenance but never used as decision-time status.
- Existing category classification is still title-based. Relationship identity
  is not.
- M1.2 may persist and invoke this core, but must not create a second live-only
  normalizer or silently fall back to title similarity.
- No watcher, polling loop, new canonical database tables, forward experiment,
  or execution behavior belongs to M1.1.

## Independent verification result

Independent verification on 2026-09-04 returned M1.1 to `IN PROGRESS`.

The ordinary correction chain and withdrawal chain reproduced from fresh DART
viewer pages, but attachment correction lineage did not. All 12 most recent
`[첨부정정]` receipts sampled from the pinned corpus caused
`DartClient.fetch_report_relations` to raise. On the representative
[DART page for `20260713000496`](https://dart.fss.or.kr/dsaf001/main.do?rcpNo=20260713000496),
the requested attachment receipt is the selected member of `att`; `family`
instead contains parent report `20260713000482` and its revision chain back to
original `20260513000801`. `_ReportRelationParser` reads only `family` and
`ref`, while `DartReportRelations` requires the requested receipt in one of
those lists. A valid provider response is consequently classified as missing
relationships, and the attachment update cannot be canonicalized with its
original event.

The passing attachment-amendment test uses a synthetic relationship in which
the requested attachment receipt appears directly in `family`; it does not
exercise the provider's `att`-to-parent structure. The corrected implementation
needs to preserve authoritative evidence for that bridge and add a captured,
provider-shaped regression.

A second provenance gap remains: `source_url` and `raw_html_sha256` are optional
on `DartReportRelations`, and the normalizer can emit `lineage_status=complete`
while every linked source has a null relationship-evidence hash. Either
evidence must be required for authoritative complete lineage or the resulting
status/documentation must distinguish an unevidenced relation.

The prevalence census independently matched 1,209,249 disclosures, 203,626
updates (16.84%), 5,281 cancellation-marker titles, and 54,526 collision
groups. The artifact regenerated byte-for-byte at `372c938c…`; focused tests
passed `23/23`, the full suite passed `214/214`, and M0.1–M0.5 hashes and
research conclusions remained unchanged. M1.2 was not started.

## Builder correction after verification failure

On 2026-09-05, the parser was extended to preserve the viewer's `att` receipt
set. The normalizer joins the requested attachment to both that set and the
parent `family` returned by the same response, while retaining a distinct
`dart_attachment` relationship in source provenance. A captured
provider-shaped fixture for `20260713000496` now proves the bridge to original
receipt `20260513000801` and preserves duplicate receipt appearances across
attachment document numbers for deduplication coverage.

Evidence is now structural rather than aspirational. `DartReportRelations`
requires an HTTPS DART viewer URL whose `rcpNo` matches the relation receipt and
a raw-response SHA-256. Linked `EventSourceProvenance` requires the same URL and
hash. A `complete` event must cover every event receipt with provenance, contain
an authoritative linked relationship, and contain no unresolved source. The
normalizer also rejects unevidenced relation objects even if ordinary schema
validation was bypassed.

All 12 attachment receipts from the failed verification parsed from fresh DART
responses. With every locally available linked disclosure supplied to the
normalizer, seven components were `complete` and five were correctly `partial`
because the local database lacked one or more viewer-linked receipts. Every
current attachment retained relationship URL/hash evidence.

The prevalence counts did not change. The artifact now describes
`family`/`att`/`ref` consistently and source-hashes the corrected generators, so
its new deterministic SHA-256 is `33ff2517…`. It regenerated byte-for-byte;
focused tests passed `26/26`, the full suite passed `217/217`, M0.5 regenerated
identically, category summaries reproduced, and M0.1–M0.5 hashes remained
unchanged. M1.1 is `AWAITING VERIFICATION`; M1.2 was not started.

## Independent re-verification

On 2026-09-07, independent fresh retrieval of the same 12 most recent local
`[첨부정정]` receipts produced no parser failures. Supplying every linked receipt
available in the pinned database reproduced seven `complete` and five `partial`
components. Receipt `20260713000496` again resolved through its `att` selector
and parent family to `20260513000801`; absent `20260706100007` and
`20260713000482` source rows kept that component truthfully partial.

Adversarial checks rejected omitted, malformed, non-DART, and receipt-mismatched
relationship evidence. Linked source provenance retained the exact viewer URL
and raw-response SHA-256. A replay check excluded future `family` and `att`
members, and all six permutations of a three-receipt attachment chain produced
identical canonical output.

The M1.1 artifact regenerated byte-for-byte at `33ff2517…`; M0.5 regenerated at
`3677a4c2…`; the M0.1–M0.4 hashes and category results stayed unchanged. Focused
tests passed `26/26`, the full suite passed `217/217`, and `git diff --check`
passed. M1.1 is accepted as `VERIFIED`; M1.2 remains `NOT STARTED`.
